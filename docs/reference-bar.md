# Komorebi House — clean-room visual and gameplay reference bar

This is a quality benchmark, not a copying brief. *Whisper of the House* is the reference for dense cutaway spaces, object tactility, and expressive decorating. The strongest DS-era top-down Pokémon games are references for immediate overworld readability, economical animation, friendly UI, and a world that invites one-more-step exploration. Komorebi House must develop its own characters, maps, props, interface, iconography, type, palette, sounds, music, names, and visual motifs.

## What the official material actually establishes

- The developer-authored [Whisper of the House Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2589500/Whisper_of_the_House/) describes a loop of town jobs → rewards and furniture → free personal-space design. It advertises more than 2,000 furniture pieces, more than 10 layouts, recolorable/retexturable surfaces, and small object-specific actions and sounds (gramophone, rubber duck, tissue box, drums, camera, tap). Its official stills and clips show non-rotating dimetric cutaways, very dense but silhouette-readable rooms, warm local light, and tiny ambient responses.
- Nintendo's official [Pokémon HeartGold page](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Games/Nintendo-DS/Pokemon-HeartGold-Version-272365.html) emphasizes updated graphics and sound plus intuitive touch access to major information. Nintendo's [Black Version page](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Games/Nintendo-DS/Pokemon-Black-Version-272332.html) and official screenshots show a tile-legible overworld that can briefly change framing for bridges, streets, and larger landmarks without losing navigation clarity. The publisher also highlighted [changing seasons](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/News/2011/A-brand-new-adventure-in-Pokemon-Black-Version-and-Pokemon-White-Version--252749.html).
- In Nintendo's developer interviews, Ken Sugimori describes resolving designs from multiple perspectives before pixel production and checking the full cast's color balance, a useful model for coherent asset direction ([Iwata Asks: “A Brand New Pokémon World”](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/ds/pokemon-black-white/0/1/)). Junichi Masuda says the games prioritize being readily understandable to someone new ([Iwata Asks: “What Pokémon Values”](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/ds/pokemon-black2-white2/0/4/)). Sugimori also identifies the series' grounded root as a child's summer in Japan rather than spectacle alone ([Iwata Asks: “The Unchanging Qualities of Pokémon”](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/ds/pokemon-black-white/0/3/)).

These sources support transferable principles; the numerical targets below are proposed clean-room production targets, not measurements of either reference game.

## Transferable benchmark

| Dimension | Komorebi House target | Reject when… |
|---|---|---|
| **Camera and composition** | Use a stable, non-rotating orthographic grammar. Room, engawa, and garden use a shallow 2:1 dimetric cutaway; village travel may open into a slightly more top-down 3/4 view, but keeps the same character scale, cardinal axes, and palette. Frame entrances, the player, and one inviting interaction without camera hunting. Reserve zoom or framing changes for location reveals and transitions. | The player is lost behind a roof or prop; depth order is ambiguous; decoration mode and walking mode look like different games; camera movement makes placement imprecise. |
| **Pixel density and asset cohesion** | Render on one logical pixel grid with nearest-neighbor scaling. At normal play size, the character, doors, seating, and usable surfaces read before decorative micro-detail. Use deliberate 1 px clusters, restrained broken outlines, 3–5-value material ramps, and consistent contact anchors. Every asset gets projection, footprint, support surface, shadow, and light-direction review before admission. | Mixed pixel sizes, fractional scaling, blurry transforms, AI-like anti-aliasing, extraction halos, pillow-shaded props, inconsistent vanishing angles, or detail that collapses into confetti at 25% thumbnail size. |
| **Lighting and atmosphere** | Establish one key-light direction per scene. Bake material-facing values into sprites, then add restrained runtime pools: warm lamp/fire/window light against cooler ambient shade, contact shadows, weather tint, and seasonal palette shifts. Light should clarify study seats and paths. Use small glints, leaf shadows, dust, rain, steam, and water movement asynchronously. | Every object appears lit from a different side; shadows are soft vector blobs against hard pixels; bloom erases clusters; the room is uniformly orange; atmosphere obscures placement cells or text. |
| **Animation cadence** | Keep camera/input/UI at 60 Hz while sprite art uses held, authored frames: generally 8–12 fps for walking and expressive prop actions, 4–8 fps for subtle loops, with occasional 1–3 px overshoot or settle. Inputs acknowledge within 100 ms; pick-up/place feedback resolves in roughly 150–300 ms. Offset ambient loops over 2–12 seconds so the room feels alive rather than synchronized. | Everything bobs continuously; motion is interpolated and mushy; props snap with no anticipation/settle; a click produces no immediate response; ambient loops all restart together. |
| **UI readability** | Follow the Pokémon lesson of newcomer-first clarity, not its visual chrome. One primary action per state; icon **and** label; strong light/dark separation; no text over noisy scenery; 44 CSS px minimum touch targets; visible keyboard/controller focus; placement ghost plus legal/illegal state; undo/redo always exposed in decorate mode. Keep item name, price, category, footprint, and owned count scannable without opening nested panels. | Pixel font is used below a readable size; color alone conveys legality; menus cover the object being placed; basic actions require memorized controls; decorative borders compete with content. |
| **Tactile interaction** | Every interactive object follows **signal → action → consequence**: hover/focus highlight or nearby prompt, immediate sound/motion, then a persistent or briefly visible state change. Placement includes lift shadow, snap, contact sound, occlusion update, collision/support validation, and undo. Prioritize delightful verbs—sit, open, switch on, pour, play, pet, water, slide, spin, write—over passive catalog volume. | A prop is visually rich but inert; identical click sound serves every material; items float or clip; shelves cannot support objects; the player cannot tell whether an action worked. |
| **Cozy customization loop** | Use a gentle loop: explore or help locally → earn currency/item/story clue → browse a clearly themed shop → preview/buy → place/recolor/use → save a room mood or study setup. Breadth must be combinatorial: floor/wall treatments, lighting, storage, seating, study gear, plants, textiles, ceramics, garden objects, and small clutter with support relationships. A polished slice should prove at least 36 finished placeables across 8 categories, 4 surface themes, day/night, and save/reload before raw catalog expansion. | Rewards are detached from decorating; items differ only by color; the best-looking props cannot be used; purchase/place/save is not one reliable loop; quantity masks inconsistent art. |
| **World rhythm** | Alternate intimate ownership (room/garden) with short, purposeful walks to a shop, café, and park. Each public place needs one unmistakable study perch, its own ambient sound bed and light mood, and 2–3 local interactions. Travel should deliver a visual or social reward every few seconds, not long empty corridors. | Locations are menu backdrops; the café/park cannot be used; routes exist only to pad time; every place shares the same palette and sound. |

## Japanese setting: spatial truth before symbols

The house should feel Japanese because of how daily life moves through it, not because generic shrine props were scattered everywhere.

- **House sequence:** a readable genkan level change and shoe storage lead to a compact lived-in room; tatami or wood modules set furniture scale; sliding partitions change privacy; an engawa forms the playable hinge between interior and garden. Japan's Cabinet Office describes the engawa as a roofed buffer between exterior shutters and interior paper doors, used for breeze, sun, tea, chores, and casual neighborhood contact ([Government of Japan: “The Merits of Traditional Japanese Houses”](https://www.gov-online.go.jp/eng/publicity/book/hlj/html/201811/201811_06_en.html)).
- **Garden sequence:** compose a path and view, not a prop pile—stepping stones, deliberately placed rocks, restrained planting, a small water or wash feature, and a framed seasonal focus. JNTO's official Kiyosumi Garden description emphasizes a pond, sculpted hills, stones, stepping paths, and a pavilion integrated with small islands ([Travel Japan / JNTO](https://www.japan.travel/en/spot/1700/)).
- **Lived-in modern mix:** pair shoji, tatami, wood, plaster, ceramic, and woven textures with a laptop, chargers, textbooks, stationery, speakers, a compact fan/heater, laundry tools, mugs, convenience-store snacks, and good task lighting. Use the kotatsu, wind chime, rain gear, mosquito coil, or seasonal bedding when the calendar makes them meaningful, not as permanent “Japan” badges.
- **Public places:** let the café read through its counter workflow, cups, menu, lighting, and customers; let the park read through paths, benches, planting, bikes, play or exercise traces, and weather. Reserve torii, offertory boxes, and other sacred markers for an actual shrine location with correct context.
- **Language:** all Japanese signage and item names must be semantically natural and reviewed by a fluent speaker. No decorative pseudo-kanji. Small regional and seasonal details should repeat coherently across architecture, plants, food, clothing, weather, and sound.

## Reference-comparison gate

Compare equal-size, unlabeled screenshots or 10-second clips; score the work, not brand resemblance.

1. **Five-second read:** can a new viewer point to the player, route/door, study seat, and likely interactable?
2. **Cohesion:** do projection, pixel size, outline logic, light direction, and material ramps feel authored by one team?
3. **Desire to touch:** does the scene make the viewer want to sit, switch something on, move an item, or explore?
4. **Customization proof:** does sparse → furnished → personally themed visibly change function and mood without breaking pathing?
5. **Japanese specificity:** does the space still read as a plausible lived-in Japanese home when obvious symbols and signage are hidden?

Pass only when all five average at least 4/5 across three reviewers and none scores below 3. Komorebi should aim to beat the references at **its own** promise—study-place comfort, room↔engawa↔garden continuity, and culturally grounded everyday detail—without being mistaken for either protected work.
